# PROJECT SUMMARY / ABSTRACT (SF424 Section 7)

**Project Title:** Development of High-Potency, Selective Ascorbate-Derived
alpha9alpha10 Nicotinic Acetylcholine Receptor Positive Allosteric Modulators
through an Integrated Medicinal Chemistry and AI-Guided Ligand-Based Approach

**Principal Investigator:** [PI NAME]
**Co-Investigator (Experimental):** [CO-PI NAME] — electrophysiology, Xenopus oocyte TEVC
**Organization:** [INSTITUTION]
**Project period:** 09/01/2027 - 08/31/2030 (3 years, 3 budget periods)

**Project Summary (public abstract):**
Nicotinic acetylcholine receptors containing alpha9alpha10 subunits are a
validated, yet chemically untouched, target for chronic pain, tinnitus, and
cochlear damage. No positive allosteric modulator (PAM) of alpha9alpha10 has
advanced into the clinic, in part because the known agonist/antagonist chemistry
(alpha4beta2, alpha7) does not transfer. We will use an integrated medicinal
chemistry + AI-guided (generative, SAR, and predictive-model) approach to
develop high-potency, selective alpha9alpha10 PAMs derived from the L-ascorbic
acid (vitamin C) pharmacophore — the only validated PAM chemotype for this
target and a selective alpha9alpha10 potentiator whose L- and D-forms are
approximately equipotent. Starting from a curated experimental dataset (30
compounds, 7 validated PAM actives, 24 sharing the ascorbate gamma-lactone
enediol core), our preliminary data show modest structural edits to ascorbic
acid dramatically enhance potency: a 3-O-substituted-5,6-acetonide analog
(ID12, 0.198 uM) and a 6-bromo-6-deoxy analog (ID25, 2.63 uM) are ~9,100-fold
and ~680-fold more potent than the validated millimolar reference. In this
aim, medicinal chemistry leads the analog program: five integrated synthesis
studies (1.1-1.5) systematically map the structural features that govern
potentiation: the C-2 hydroxyl (Study 1.1),
conformationally rigid bicyclic analogs (Study 1.2), C-3-O-heterocyclic
analogs (Study 1.3), the C-5/C-6 hydroxyls (Study 1.4), and deoxy-ascorbate
mimics/azalactams (Study 1.5). Computational/AI tools supplement that
medicinal-chemistry program by prioritizing which planned analogs to advance.
The approach is innovative in its dual focus: systematic ascorbate SAR to
define the structural requirements for
alpha9alpha10 PAM activity, and strategic design of transporter-compatible
analogs to facilitate targeted cochlear delivery, enhancing the potential for
dual-acting compounds that are both potent PAMs and cochlea-deliverable.
Newly synthesized and AI-proposed analogs will be evaluated iteratively by
two-
electrode voltage-clamp electrophysiology on Xenopus oocytes (co-PI
laboratory) in pre-registered, comparator-arm rounds (enrichment factor >=2.0,
BH-FDR alpha=0.10), with an oversight gate requiring medicinal-chemistry
sign-off before any experiment. Every round refits a predictive model of
alpha9alpha10 PAM activity (last round held out) to guide the next round of
design. Two in-loop design axes keep the effort honest and reproducible: a
phase-4b selectivity benchmark scores each analog against a curated, isolated
panel of 70 non-alpha9alpha10 nicotinic modulators, and phase-4c
retrosynthetic route prediction (AIZynthFinder) is scaffolded as a pipeline
phase that will attach
PREDICTED_ROUTE/UNVERIFIED synthesis plans plus a building-block stock screen
to every candidate (the phase is NOT yet live: AIZynthFinder is not installed,
0/7 round-1 targets are routed, and it self-reports NOT AVAILABLE — no routes
are fabricated) — so both selectivity and synthesizability are reviewed against
reproducible evidence as soon as the phase is installed. This project delivers the first experimentally
enumerated, high-potency and selective ascorbate-derived alpha9alpha10 PAM
series, with a reusable, adaptive AI-guided ligand-design platform that
supports (not replaces) the medicinal-chemistry program.

**Relevance statement (for the public):**
This project uses traditional chemistry knowledge combined with computer
"design assistants" to develop and test new vitamin C-based chemical families
that safely modulate a receptor implicated in chronic pain and hearing loss,
and converts each round of results into shared chemical knowledge
that speeds up future drug discovery.
